European Job Market Visualizer github.com/alexandrucruceanu/EU-jobs

🗺 Map

This is a research tool that visualizes over 400 occupations from the ESCO framework, covering the European job market. Each rectangle's area is proportional to total employment. Color shows the selected metric — toggle between projected growth outlook, median pay, education requirements, and AI exposure. Click any tile or node to view its detailed profile and ESCO data.

LLM-powered coloring: The pipeline scores each occupation via Gemini. The "Digital AI Exposure" metric estimates how much current AI will reshape each occupation based on routine digital knowledge processing vs hands-on real-world interaction.

View the Digital AI Exposure scoring prompt (example)
You are an expert analyst evaluating how exposed different occupations are to AI. You will be given a detailed description of an occupation from the ESCO framework (European Skills, Competences, Qualifications and Occupations). Rate the occupation's overall AI Exposure on a scale from 0 to 10. AI Exposure measures: how much will AI reshape this occupation? Consider both direct effects (AI automating tasks currently done by humans) and indirect effects (AI making each worker so productive that fewer are needed). - 0–1: Minimal exposure. Hands-on physical work. - 2–3: Low exposure. Mostly physical/interpersonal. - 4–5: Moderate exposure. Mix of physical & knowledge work. - 6–7: High exposure. Predominantly knowledge work. - 8–9: Very high exposure. Pure computer/digital knowledge tasks. - 10: Maximum exposure. Routine information processing.

Caveat on Digital AI Exposure scores: These are rough LLM estimates, not rigorous predictions. A high score does not predict the job will disappear. Software developers score 9/10 because AI is transforming their work — but demand for software could easily grow as each developer becomes more productive.

Data Methodology: Total Employment reflects exact Eurostat census figures. Growth, Pay, and Education metrics are algorithmic approximations imputed directly from official Eurostat 2-digit broad category averages.
